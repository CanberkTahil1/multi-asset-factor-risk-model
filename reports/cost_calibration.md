## 1. The two square-root prefactors, backed out of published figures

SPEC.md 7.2: AQR's ~10bp and Virtu's ~40bp are **both correct** and measure
different things -- one patient, liquidity-providing manager against a peer
universe that includes urgent and event-driven flow. Both regimes are modelled;
neither is selected, and there are no interior points (operator ruling,
2026-09-03, W5-P1: they are calibrated regimes, not endpoints of a continuum).

Under the square-root law the cost of a trade, as a proportion of the trade, is

```
cost/|Q| = Y * sigma * (Q/V)^(e-1)        e = 1.5, so (Q/V)^0.5 = sqrt(Q/V)
Y        = cost / (sigma * sqrt(Q/V))
```

Every anchor is quoted at sigma = 2%/day.

| Regime | Source | Participation Q/V | Published cost | sigma * sqrt(Q/V) | Implied `Y` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| patient | AQR, Craftsmanship Alpha (live execution data) | 2% of ADV | 17bp | 0.002828 | **0.601** |
| patient | AQR, Craftsmanship Alpha (live execution data) | 6% of ADV | 28bp | 0.004899 | **0.572** |
| urgent | Virtu Global Cost Review, US trailing-year average | 2% of ADV | 40bp | 0.002828 | **1.414** |

| Regime | Mean of implied `Y` | `costs.square_root_prefactor` | Rounding |
|---|---|---|---|
| patient | 0.5863 | **0.58** | -0.0063 |
| urgent | 1.4142 | **1.40** | -0.0142 |

Ratio urgent / patient: **2.41x**.

**Cross-check** (SPEC.md 7.2): at Q/V = 1% and sigma = 2%/day, `Y_patient` = 0.58 gives **11.60bp** of the trade against the spec's 11.6bp -- consistent with AQR's value-weighted market impact of ~15bp at a mean order size of 1.2% of ADV.

Both `Y` values are **borrowed coefficients**: this project has no execution data,
so any impact coefficient is inherited from a published figure rather than
estimated. The README's Limitations section names them as such.

## 2. Scaling laws (SPEC.md 7.3)

At `costs.total_cost_exponent` = 1.5, with the portfolio structure fixed:

| Quantity | Scales as | Exponent |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per unit traded | `A^(e-1)` | 0.5 |
| Annual drag, bps of AUM | `tau * A^(e-1)` | 0.5 on `A`, 1 on `tau` |
| Annual cost, dollars | `tau * A^e` | 1.5 |
| Cost vs volatility | linear | 1 |
| Cost vs ADV | `V^-(e-1)` | -0.5 |

Doubling AUM raises the bps drag by **1.41x**, not 2x; doubling turnover raises it by **2.00x**. Turnover is the linear lever and size the sublinear one. Cost is linear in
volatility, so a model without a vol term under-charges in stressed markets --
exactly when the optimizer most wants to trade (H5).

## 3. The spread: level from the issuer, shape from EDGE

SPEC.md 3.4.1: EDGE supplies time variation and cannot supply level for this
sleeve. The per-asset `a_i` of SPEC.md 7.1 is therefore built as

```
s_i(t) = s_issuer,i + max(0, s_EDGE,i(t) - baseline_i)     baseline_i = in-sample median of clipped EDGE
a_i(t) = s_i(t) / 2 + commission                              commission = 0 (no published figure)
```

Additive, not multiplicative: for the liquid names the clipped EDGE baseline is
often exactly zero, and a ratio against it is the division-by-zero class SPEC.md
7.4 names. A level offset cancels in a widening (SPEC.md 3.4.1 consequence 1).
The issuer figure is a 2026 disclosure applied to 2009-2024 -- the anachronism
SPEC.md 3.4.1 accepted when it made issuer medians the level source. The five
curve points are priced through tradable proxies; each proxy's cost is the cost
of trading the ETF, not the exposure the risk model priced, and **the 30y proxy
(TLT) has roughly half the duration of a 30y zero, so its cost is the cost of
trading about half that exposure.**

`Negative before clip` is the resolution diagnostic of SPEC.md 3.4: near 50% means
the estimator is reporting noise around zero on a tight instrument, which is the
expected reading for every liquid name here and is why the level is not EDGE's.

## 4. What is swept and what is not

- `Y`: the two regimes above, 0.58 and 1.40. Both always, no interior points.
- `gamma_trade`: 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3 -- half-steps from 1 to 3, stated as the rule. Characterising the cost model across the grid is `data-diagnostic`; selecting a point on backtest
  performance in W6 is `strategy-config` and counts in the deflated-Sharpe trial
  count then (registered in `experiments.md`).
- The spread level: a point per asset from the issuer file, or a sourced band
  swept and reported as a range for any asset whose issuer discloses no median.
- No separate no-trade band exists or will be added: the `a|z|` L1 term already
  generates the no-trade region, and a band on top double-counts (SPEC.md 7.1).

## 5. What the issuers' display resolution means for the identity

Every issuer displays its median bid/ask spread at 0.01% resolution, so a displayed figure is a rounding interval of the full spread 1bp wide, and the
half-spread `a_i` is known to +-0.25bp. The cost model runs at both ends of that
interval -- the same code path as a sourced band for a missing level -- and no midpoint
is invented. **The band below is the honest width of that uncertainty, not a sensitivity
anyone chose.**

At the anchor volatility of 2%/day with `Y_patient`, per-unit impact is **16.4bp** at 2% of ADV and **3.67bp** at 0.1%. A 0.5bp half-spread is therefore **3.0%** of the impact term on the large trade and **13.6%** on the small one: the 1bp resolution matters
for small trades and is negligible for large ones.

**`commodity` (DBC) has both the widest spread band ([2.5, 3.5)bp) and the thinnest volume ($31M/day median), so for any given dollar trade both terms of the cost function are largest there -- the spread term by its level and the impact term through participation. That is coherent: the least liquid instrument in the sleeve
is the least liquid on both measures at once.**

The 3% figure above is at the anchor's 2%/day. The bond sleeve trades at a tenth to a third of that volatility, so its impact term is correspondingly smaller and the same 0.5bp is a first-order share of it even on the large trade: `SHY` 122%, `IEI` 43%, `IEF` 23%, `TIP` 27%, `LQD` 24%, `HYG` 21%. For those assets the cost term of the identity is spread-dominated, and the display resolution is the binding uncertainty on it rather than a rounding detail.

Caveats carried from section 3: the five curve points are priced through proxies, the 30y
through TLT at roughly half a 30y zero's duration; every level is a 2026 disclosure applied
to the in-sample window.
