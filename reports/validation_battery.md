# The validation battery: SPEC.md 6.5 on every covariance variant

Generated by `python -m mafrm.factors.battery_report`. SPEC.md 6.5, W4-P3.

SPEC.md 6.1's bias statistic is one number per portfolio. The battery asks the questions that number cannot: is the model wrong *conditionally* (Mincer-Zarnowitz), did volatility clustering survive the forecast (Ljung-Box), is the VaR level right (Kupiec) and do the breaches cluster (Christoffersen), what would a regulator's traffic light say (Basel), is expected shortfall calibrated (Acerbi-Szekely), and does the model rank a risky asset above a safe one at all. Every test runs on the **same** series the headline `B` is computed from -- family 4's, the minimum-variance portfolio rebuilt each date, pre-VRA by SPEC.md 6.2.2 -- for every variant of the attribution ladder, so a column can be read down as an attribution and a row across as a profile.

## What was scored, and under which rulings

- scored window: 3,874 of 3,882 forecast dates, 2009-05-07 to 2024-12-31; dropped 7 before the last PSD-repair firing, 0 where T <= N = 13 left the naive comparand singular, and 1 with no SPEC.md 5.4 multiplier yet; the same window at both horizons.
- Series: `b_t = R_t / sigma_t` for family 4, **unclipped** (3,874 daily observations). SPEC.md 6.1's `+-4` clip belongs to its standard deviation only; a VaR test on a clipped series would have had its tail removed by hand. The `B` column is the clipped SPEC.md 6.1 statistic from `reports/bias_statistics.md`, as the anchor.
- **Ruling 1**: VaR and ES at 95% and 99%, both reported, neither selected. At `T = 3,874` the 99% level expects 39 breaches and the 95% level 194.
- **Ruling 2**: the tests use the normal -- `VaR = z sigma`, `ES = sigma phi(z)/alpha` -- because it is the distribution the variance forecast implicitly assumes. The 'tail ratio' column is the diagnostic that replaces a fitted tail: the standardised returns' own lower quantile over the normal's, above one when the tail is fatter than the forecast assumes. It is never fed into a test.
- **Ruling 3**: Ljung-Box at `h` = 21, 63, 252 -- a month, a quarter, a year -- all three reported.
- **Ruling 4**: the Acerbi-Szekely null is simulated with `M = 2,000`, read from `eigenfactor.monte_carlo_trials` through a pointer; p-values are one-sided in the direction of understatement, resolution 1/M.
- **Ruling 5**: **Menchero & Ji's Q-statistic is not implemented.** The repository does not hold *JPM* 50(3) 2024 and SPEC.md 6.5 gives one sentence, so any construction here would claim a provenance it does not have. The 'rank rho' column is this project's own plain cross-sectional Spearman between the forecast volatility standing when a block opened and the realised standard deviation inside it, on **non-overlapping** 21-day blocks (184 of them, so that is the `n`), across the 11 assets that are not construction artefacts. It answers the same diagnostic question and is never called Q.
- Basel: 250-day **non-overlapping** blocks at 99%, green <= 4, yellow to 9, red above; 15 blocks and a 124-day unscored tail.
- Newey-West lags for Mincer-Zarnowitz and the factor t-statistics: 5, read from `covariance.volatility_newey_west_lags`.

## What the battery adds to the bias statistic

**Read the Basel and Mincer-Zarnowitz rows as one finding, in the vocabulary a risk interviewer speaks.** This model would fail a regulatory backtest in a plurality of years: at a 99% VaR over 250-day windows the pre-VRA factor model is in the **red zone in 7 of 15** blocks at the short horizon and 6 of 15 at the long, against 2 of 15 for the naive covariance on the same portfolio. Its Mincer-Zarnowitz slope is **2.6 short / 2.9 long**: realised variance rises two-and-a-half to three times faster than the forecast, so the model is not merely low on average but low in proportion to how much risk there is. Neither is a failed test in its own right. Both are SPEC.md 6.2.6's specification error -- the diagonal `Delta` in `Sigma = X F X' + Delta`, which discards residual correlations that scale with the level of risk -- expressed as a regulatory outcome and as a conditional-calibration coefficient. The cause is an assumption the specification makes, not an estimate the data failed to pin down, and no amount of history repairs it.

**The two-level ruling paid for itself on the naive comparand.** `sample` passes Kupiec at 95% (`p` = 0.27 short) and fails at 99%: its `B` = 1.066 is a **tail** excess (tail ratio 1.03 at 95%, 1.28 at 99%), which either level alone would have hidden -- one would have reported a calibrated model, the other a miscalibrated one, and neither would have said the disagreement was the finding.

- **The level error `B` measures is everywhere.** On the pre-VRA factor model (`eigen_a1`, short) Kupiec rejects at both levels (377 breaches against 194 expected at 95%; 177 against 39 at 99%), the Mincer-Zarnowitz slope is 2.57, and 7 of 15 Basel blocks are red.
- **The naive comparand is better calibrated on family 4, and it is not close.** `sample` (short) has `B = 1.0660`, 85 breaches at 99%, 8 green blocks of 15, and QLIKE 3.5146 against `eigen_a1`'s 4.1596. That is SPEC.md 6.2.6 seen through six more instruments: the diagonal specific-risk assumption, not sampling error, is what the factor model pays for on optimiser-selected portfolios.
- **The tail is fatter than any `B` explains.** Tail ratios at 99% run 1.28-1.77 across every variant and horizon: dividing by `B` would still leave the normal's 1% quantile inside the empirical one. A Student-t was refused (ruling 2) because its `nu` would be fitted on this very series; what stands instead is the number.
- **Breaches cluster.** Christoffersen's independence test at 95% rejects on 8 of 9 short-horizon variants, and Ljung-Box on `b^2` rejects at every lag on every pre-VRA factor variant: an 84-day half-life does not track the clustering, which is the timing error SPEC.md 5.4's VRA exists for -- and the `vra_*` rows show what it buys.
- **The model does rank risk.** The cross-sectional Spearman is positive on every variant at more than two standard errors over non-overlapping monthly blocks, which is the one question in this battery the factor model answers well. A model can have `B = 1.00` and fail this; this one has `B = 1.33` and passes it.

Nothing here changes a configuration. Every row in this table is one of `experiments.md` rows 144-161 re-scored, and rows 187-188 record that.
